About me · An engineering life

<{{ about_heading_tag }} id="{{ about_title_id }}">It started with a robot.

I’m Mehrdad Zakershahrak, also known as Mehrdad Zaker. For roughly twenty years, I’ve followed the same curiosity: how can an idea become something that works in the world?

Shiraz · Beginning in 2006

Learning by making things move

I joined a robotics team at Shiraz University while studying computer engineering. The small humanoid robots in these photographs brought software, electronics, vision, and movement into the same problem. That was what drew me in: designing something, trying it on real hardware, and finding out what needed to change.

After finishing my bachelor’s in 2010, I started a business selling automatic openers for gates and garage doors. It was an early move from university projects to useful everyday automation, and my first experience building a company around a practical engineering product.

Graduate study · 2010–2016

Following the idea into the hardware

After a master’s in information technology at K. N. Toosi University of Technology, I moved to the United States and studied computer science at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln. My work there included machine learning and computer vision algorithms on FPGAs, continuing the connection between algorithms and hardware that had interested me in robotics.

My Nebraska thesis focused on secure, lightweight hardware authentication using physically unclonable functions. Alongside the vision work, it gave me another way to think about a complete system: both what it can do and what makes its underlying hardware trustworthy.

Arizona State University · 2016–2021

Bringing people into the system

My Ph.D. widened the question from how a machine behaves to how people and intelligent systems work together. My dissertation, Towards Human-Machine Symbiosis: Design for Effective AI Facilitation, explored that broader relationship. Robotics was one part of it: a concrete setting for studying planning, explanation, and collaboration.

I developed methods for explaining an AI system’s plans progressively, with attention to the person trying to understand them. Alongside that research, I built prototypes for autonomous navigation, robot mapping, VR control of a UR5 arm, and multi-agent planning. The papers and the working systems were complementary ways to investigate the same ideas.

Grainger · 2021–2024

Engineering for everyday use

As a Staff Applied ML Scientist at Grainger, I worked on production search, ranking, and recommendation systems. I also explored 3D vision and robotic automation for distribution centers and co-invented a robotic vending system, later granted as a U.S. patent. The problems ranged from helping someone find a product to designing how a machine could retrieve it.

Neural Intelligence Labs · 2025–Present

Building a company, still building things

I founded Neural Intelligence Labs to bring research and product engineering together. As Founder / Member of Technical Staff, I work directly on the systems: Hormuz, an open-source gateway for managing access, budgets, and privacy around AI coding tools; IDX, for working with documents and their sources; and Fixo, an agentic technical support workflow.

The medium has expanded from robots and electronics to learning systems, software tools, and apps. I still enjoy the same part of the work: finding a problem worth solving, designing a possible answer, and staying with it through the details that make it useful.
