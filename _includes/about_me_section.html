I’m Mehrdad Zaker. For roughly twenty years, I’ve followed the same curiosity: how can an idea become something that works in the world?

Teaching a robot to see and find its way

Shiraz University, beginning in 2006

At Shiraz University, I joined a team competing in the RoboCup Humanoid KidSize league. I built the robot’s vision and path-planning algorithms and implemented them on its onboard hardware. Seeing the code guide a physical machine made engineering feel immediate: every design choice had to work on the robot.

After my bachelor’s in 2010, I started a business selling automatic openers for gates and garage doors. It was my first company, built around a practical use for automation.

From robot hardware to embedded learning

Graduate study, 2010 to 2016

That experience became a foundation for implementing machine learning models on FPGAs and embedded devices. After a master’s at K. N. Toosi University of Technology, I moved to the United States for a computer science master’s at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln. My work included ML and vision on FPGAs; my thesis focused on secure hardware authentication using physically unclonable functions.

Bringing people into the system

Arizona State University, 2016 to 2021

My Ph.D. expanded that interest into human–AI interaction: how people understand, guide, and collaborate with intelligent systems. I developed methods for explaining AI plans progressively, with attention to the person receiving the explanation. Robotics provided one setting within that broader research, alongside work on planning and learning.

I also built working prototypes for navigation, manipulation, and interaction. These two demos show that side of the work.

Engineering for everyday use

Production ML and robotics, 2021 to 2024

As a Staff Applied ML Scientist, I built production search, ranking, and recommendation systems. I also explored 3D vision and robotic automation for distribution centers and co-invented a robotic vending system. The problems ranged from helping someone find a product to designing how a machine could retrieve it.

Building a company, still building things

Neural Intelligence Labs, 2025 to present

I founded Neural Intelligence Labs to bring research and product engineering together. As Founder / Member of Technical Staff, I work directly on tools that make AI useful in everyday workflows.

Hormuz

I built Hormuz, a gateway for access policies, spending limits, and secret redaction in AI coding tools such as Codex and Claude Code.

Explore Hormuz

IDX

I designed the architecture for Index, connecting agent workflows to PDF parsing and structured JSON. Its document tools also connect answers to their sources.

Read about Index

Fixo

Coordinate tools to resolve technical issues. I built an Azure support workflow connecting more than 40 tools.

My recent projects also include Koto, a workspace for coding agents, and LinkedFull, an iPhone app for finding clothing from visual inspiration.

I still enjoy following an idea through the details that make it useful, whether the result is a robot, an embedded model, or a software tool.
